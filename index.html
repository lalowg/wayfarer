          Plate IVRoute ledger

          Your route, leg by leg.

          Every place you save becomes a stop. The ledger measures each leg along the great circle, the shortest path over the globe, with the compass bearing you would set off on.

          No stops yet. Open a place on the atlas and choose Save to route, or save every stop of a collection.

          Plate VReading the atlas

          A key to the map.

          Place
          One of 25 places in the atlas. Select it to read a culture note.
          Saved stop
          A place in your route, numbered in the order you will visit.
          Your route
          Great-circle legs between saved stops, drawn in vermilion ink.
          Collection
          A curated line, dotted in teal when you plot it.
          Water lines
          Engraved ripples that follow every coastline, after old copperplate charts.

          Projection. The atlas uses the Equal Earth projection, published in 2018, which keeps every country at its true relative area. Africa looks as large as it really is next to Europe, and the poles do not balloon.

          Data. Coastlines and borders come from Natural Earth at 1:110 million scale, through the open world-atlas dataset. At this scale small islands are simplified or omitted, and borders are shown for orientation only.
